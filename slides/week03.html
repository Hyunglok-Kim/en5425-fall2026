Part 1 · 15 min

The Data Zoo

15 minutes. Goal: not memorizing spec sheets — building the reflex of asking four questions of any product: what does it measure, at what spacing, how often, how late, and how wrong. Transition: start with why this matters for models, not just for data hygiene.

Part 1 · 15 min

The corpus is the prior

  • A pretrained model can only know what its corpus sampled: coverage, cadence, spectral content, and error character become the model's inductive prior.
  • Prithvi saw HLS over the US → global transfer is an open question; Prithvi-WxC saw 160 MERRA-2 variables → it inherits MERRA-2's biases.
  • Every headline weather model — FourCastNet, Pangu, GraphCast, GenCast — is an ERA5 emulator first.
  • So "which dataset?" is a modeling decision, not a logistics one.
Jakubik et al. 2023 (Prithvi) · Schmude et al. 2024 (Prithvi-WxC) · Rasp et al. 2024 (WeatherBench 2)
2 min. Frame hard: students often treat data choice as plumbing. Point out that a model trained purely on ERA5 has never seen an observation — it has seen a model's opinion of observations. That lands the whole lecture. Transition: so let's meet the animals.

Part 1 · 15 min

Processing level = how much model is in the product

  • L1 calibrated instrument measurements (radiances, backscatter) · L2 retrieved geophysical variable, swath geometry · L3 gridded/composited L2 · L4 obs assimilated into a model.
  • SMAP L3: a retrieval — gaps where the satellite didn't pass, flags where retrieval fails.
  • SMAP L4: gap-free, 3-hourly, root-zone — because a land-surface model fills the space between overpasses.
  • Gap-free is not free: you are training on model physics wherever there was no overpass.
SMAP L3 = SPL3SMP · SMAP L4 = SPL4SMGP (NASA Catchment model + EnKF)
2 min. The one-liner to repeat: "every level up trades instrument error for model error." Ask the class: for training an FM on soil moisture, which do you want — honest gaps (L3) or seamless model blend (L4)? There's no universal answer; it depends whether the model should learn observation structure or a complete state. Transition: this question explodes for ERA5.

Part 1 · 15 min

Reanalysis ≠ observation ≠ analysis

  • Observation — what an instrument measured: irregular, gappy, instrument error.
  • Analysis — best estimate of the current state: observations assimilated into a forecast model; the operational system keeps changing.
  • Reanalysis — decades of analyses recomputed with one frozen model + DA system.
  • ERA5 = IFS Cy41r2 (2016-era) + 4D-Var, hourly, 0.25°, 137 levels, 1940–present.
  • Consistent physics ≠ consistent accuracy: the observing system underneath still changes (satellite era ≫ pre-1979).
Hersbach et al. 2020, QJRMS (ERA5) · ERA5T preliminary stream runs ~5 days behind real time
3 min — this is the conceptual core of Part 1. Walk the loop: the model forecasts, observations correct it, the corrected state seeds the next forecast. A reanalysis replays this loop for 80 years without upgrading the model, so trends aren't artifacts of software upgrades. Punchline for this course: when we "evaluate against ERA5," we evaluate against this loop's output — a model product. That is DQ2 in the reading discussion; plant it now.

Part 1 · 15 min

Error character: every product has a personality

  • IMERG Early (4 h) → Late (14 h) → Final (~3.5 mo, gauge-corrected): pick freshness or quality — never train on Final and deploy on Early without testing the gap.
  • Optical (MODIS, S2, HLS): clouds are structured missingness — correlated with rain, i.e. with the events you care about.
  • SAR (S1): all-weather but speckled; backscatter lives in dB (log) space.
  • Reanalysis: smooth and gap-free by construction — variance is too low, extremes are damped.
Error structure is rarely i.i.d. — it correlates with the phenomena being predicted
2–3 min. Hammer the cloud point: missingness in optical EO is not random — it co-occurs with precipitation, so a flood-mapping model trained on cloud-free optical chips has a censored view of floods. That's why Sen1Floods11 (W4 lab) exists. Check time: should be ~13 min in. Transition: knowing what to read is half; now how to read petabytes without downloading them.

Part 2 · 15 min

Cloud-Native Geospatial

15 minutes: three formats (COG, Zarr, STAC), one mechanism (chunking), one map of where everything lives. The thesis: the format decides your training throughput before your model runs a single step.

Part 2 · 15 min

Download-then-process is dead

  • ERA5 at 0.25°/hourly is petabyte-scale; the Sentinel-2 archive is tens of PB — no one downloads the corpus anymore.
  • Cloud-native flips the model: data sits in object storage (S3/GCS); you issue HTTP range requests for exactly the bytes you need.
  • Three specs make that possible: COG (images), Zarr (N-D arrays), STAC (search).
  • Throughput currency: useful bytes ÷ bytes moved. Everything today optimizes that ratio.
2 min. Define "cloud-native" precisely: a format is cloud-native when a remote reader can fetch a small piece without reading the whole file — layout + metadata designed for partial reads over HTTP. Write the ratio on the board; the chunking slide quantifies it.

Part 2 · 15 min

COG — Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFF

  • A completely valid GeoTIFF, internally organized: tiled layout (e.g. 512×512 blocks) + overview pyramids + metadata at the front of the file.
  • A reader fetches the header, learns where each tile's bytes live, then range-requests only the tiles under its window.
  • Overviews serve zoomed-out views for free — same file powers browsing and training.
  • One object, many concurrent windowed readers; no server-side code — plain object storage.
Sentinel-2 L2A on AWS Earth Search is distributed as COGs · readers: rasterio/GDAL, rioxarray, stackstac
2 min. Key mental model: COG turns "an image file" into "a byte-addressable tile database." Reading a 64×64 chip from an 11,000×11,000-pixel scene costs a header read plus one or two tile reads — a few hundred KB, not the ~1 GB scene. This is exactly what stackstac does under the hood in today's lab.

Part 2 · 15 min

Zarr — chunked N-D arrays in object storage

  • An N-D array cut into chunks; each chunk is one compressed object; JSON metadata describes shape, dtype, chunking, compression.
  • Chunks are addressed by index (t.0.3.7) → massively parallel reads, no file server.
  • xarray.open_zarr maps it to labeled dims/coords; consolidated metadata = one round-trip to open a PB-scale store.
  • Zarr is to arrays what COG is to images — and it is how ERA5 is actually served for ML.
zarr-python · xarray · gcsfs/s3fs for the storage layer
2 min. Contrast with NetCDF/HDF5: same data model (labeled N-D arrays), but classic HDF5 assumed a local filesystem — remote partial reads are painful. Zarr's move: metadata in plain JSON, each chunk its own object. Opening WeatherBench2's ERA5 store takes seconds because metadata is consolidated into one JSON document.

Part 2 · 15 min

STAC — the search index over it all

  • SpatioTemporal Asset Catalog: an Item = one scene — geometry, datetime, properties, and assets (links to the actual COGs); Items group into Collections.
  • The STAC API answers: bbox + datetime + property filters (e.g. eo:cloud_cover < 20) → a list of Items.
  • pystac-client searches; stackstac/odc-stac turn the result into a lazy xarray datacube.
  • STAC finds the scenes; COG serves the pixels; Zarr holds what you keep.
Earth Search (Element 84) on AWS: Sentinel-2 L2A · Copernicus Data Space and NASA CMR also speak STAC
2 min. The last bullet is the sentence to remember — the three specs are one pipeline, not three competing formats. In the lab, the chip pipe is literally: STAC query → filter by cloud metadata → stackstac windowed COG reads → write local chip store.

Part 2 · 15 min

Chunk layout dominates throughput

  • Cost of a read = chunks touched × chunk size — the store decompresses whole chunks, never single values.
  • Layout A is perfect for "one global map" and catastrophic for time series; layout B is the mirror image.
  • Rule: chunk so your dominant access pattern — for training, the sample shape — touches few chunks.
3–4 min, the mechanism slide of Part 2. Quantify: ERA5 T2m is 721×1440 float32 ≈ 4 MB per hourly step. In layout A, a 40-year hourly point time series touches ~350,000 chunks — you move ~1.4 TB to keep 1.4 MB: efficiency 10⁻⁶. Same data, same store tech, only the chunk map changed. This is DQ3 — chunking as a scientific issue: whether a climatology, a time-series analysis, or a training epoch is feasible is decided at write time by whoever chunked the archive. WB2 publishes multiple chunkings of the same ERA5 for exactly this reason.

Part 3 · 15 min

ERA5 as a Pretraining Corpus

15 minutes, the most mathematical part of today: the grid, the cos-latitude weight (derived, not asserted), anomalies vs climatology, and regridding. All four reappear in Weeks 9–11 as the machinery under GraphCast and WeatherBench2 scores.

Part 3 · 15 min

ERA5 is the ImageNet of weather

  • FourCastNet, Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, GenCast: all trained on ERA5 at 0.25° — one corpus, one field's worth of models.
  • WeatherBench 2 fixes the protocol: train ≤ 2018, test 2020 — temporal holdout, exactly the W2 leakage lesson.
  • State vector: surface (T2m, U10/V10, MSLP) + upper-air variables on 13 pressure levels (50 → 1000 hPa); Z500 is the headline — geopotential height at 500 hPa tracks the midlatitude synoptic pattern.
  • "Beats IFS" always means: beats IFS at reproducing ERA5's version of the atmosphere.
Bi et al. 2023 (Pangu) · Lam et al. 2023 (GraphCast) · Rasp et al. 2024 (WB2)
3 min. Explain why Z500 of all things: it's smooth, dynamically central (ridges/troughs = weather regimes), and the historical NWP skill metric — continuity back to Bauer et al.'s "quiet revolution." The last bullet plants DQ2 again: the ceiling of this whole enterprise is ERA5's own fidelity. Lab pipe (a) computes a Z500 climatology — today they touch the exact variable the field scores itself on.

Part 3 · 15 min

The equiangular grid oversamples the poles

  • 0.25° equiangular lat–lon grid → a 721 × 1440 array; every row has 1440 cells.
  • But physical cell width shrinks with latitude: area ∝ cos φ.
  • cos 0° = 1 vs cos 89.875° ≈ 0.0022 → an equator cell covers ~450× the area of a near-pole cell.
  • The array is a lie about the sphere — any unweighted mean over it inherits the lie.
2–3 min. Point at the figure: every row holds the same number of array cells, but the 80° row is a sliver of real estate. So in the flat array, polar air is stored ~450 times more densely per unit area than tropical air. Ask: what happens to a naive global-mean temperature? (Poles are cold → biased cold.) Don't give the number — the lab measures it. Transition: let's make the fix exact, not hand-wavy.

Part 3 · 15 min

Deriving the cos-latitude weighted mean

  • Area element on a sphere: dA = R² · cos φ · dφ · dλ — the cos φ is the shrinking circumference of a latitude circle.
  • For a grid cell at latitude φᵢ with fixed Δφ, Δλ: Aᵢ ≈ R² · cos φᵢ · Δφ · Δλ → everything cancels but wᵢ = cos φᵢ.
  • Area-true global mean of a field xᵢⱼ (i = lat row, j = lon column):

⟨x⟩ = Σᵢ Σⱼ cos φᵢ · xᵢⱼ  /  ( Nλ · Σᵢ cos φᵢ )

  • One line of code: ds.weighted(np.cos(np.deg2rad(ds.lat))).mean(("lat","lon")) — but now you know why.
  • Same weight sits inside WB2's headline metric, latitude-weighted RMSE — and inside ACC (W11).
Exact per-row weight uses Δ(sin φ) across cell edges; cos φᵢ is its midpoint approximation — fine at 0.25°
4 min — do the derivation live on the board, it's three lines: sphere area element → cell area → constants cancel. Emphasize the logical order: the weight is not a convention, it falls out of geometry. Mention the footnote honestly: the exact discrete weight is proportional to sin(φ_north edge) − sin(φ_south edge); cos φ at the midpoint is the small-Δφ approximation everyone uses. Lab pipe (a) verifies weighted vs naive T2m mean — students must predict the sign of the difference before running it.

Part 3 · 15 min

Anomalies: subtract the boring part

  • Climatology c = the expected state for this location, day-of-year, hour — averaged over a fixed reference period (WB2: 1990–2019).
  • Anomaly: x′ = x − c — what remains is weather, after the seasonal and diurnal cycles are removed.
  • Why it matters: predicting "warm in August" is free skill; anomaly metrics refuse to pay for it.
  • Sets up W11: ACC = Σ w·x′f·x′o / √(Σ w·x′f² · Σ w·x′o²) — the anomaly correlation coefficient, with the same cos φ weights; ACC ≈ 0.6 is the classic "useful forecast" line.
ACC compares forecast anomaly x′f with observed anomaly x′o — climatology scores exactly 0
3 min. The rhetorical move: a model that memorizes the seasonal cycle looks great on raw RMSE at long leads. Anomaly space is where skill claims become honest — climatology itself scores zero ACC by construction. Lab pipe (a) builds a Z500 climatology and anomaly field, so this slide becomes muscle memory today. Note normalization parallel: climatology is also computed on reference years only — same train-only discipline as normalization stats, coming in Part 4.

Part 3 · 15 min

Regridding: conservative vs bilinear

Bilinear

  • Interpolates point values from the 4 surrounding nodes; smooth output.
  • Right for smooth, intensive state fields: T, Z, wind — especially going finer.
  • Does not preserve integrals — it will quietly create or destroy total rainfall.

Conservative

  • Weights by cell-overlap areas; preserves area integrals (mass, energy, total precip).
  • Right for fluxes and precipitation, and for any coarsening (0.25° → 5.625° WB2 subsets).
  • Going finer it gives blocky fields — correct totals, ugly maps.
  • Categorical fields (SCL, land cover): nearest-neighbor only — you cannot average class 4 and class 6. Tools: xesmf/ESMF.
3 min. Decision rule to dictate: "Is the quantity a point sample of a smooth field, or an amount per area? Point → bilinear. Amount → conservative. Class label → nearest." Concrete failure: bilinearly regrid IMERG to 0.25° and continental total rainfall changes — a physics bug, not a rendering bug. The 5.625° ERA5 they use in lab was produced conservatively; they are consumers of this choice today. Time check: ~45 min in.

Part 4 · 10 min

Dataset Objects for Pretraining

10 minutes. From archives to __getitem__: what a sample IS (chips, tiles, pixel series), and the two curation steps every real FM corpus lives on — normalization and QA masking.

Part 4 · 10 min

What is a training sample? Chips, tiles, pixel series

  • The choice couples to architecture: ViT-MAE models eat chips (SatMAE 224², Prithvi); Presto (~400K params) and TESSERA eat pixel series; tiles are for delivery and inference tiling, never for batching.
Cong et al. 2022 (SatMAE) · Tseng et al. 2023 (Presto) · lab chips: 64×64 × 4 bands
3 min. Vocabulary slide — nail the three words because papers blur them. A tile is what the agency ships; a chip is what your dataloader yields; a pixel series is the degenerate chip that turns EO into sequence modeling. Note the economy argument: Presto proves a 400K-param model on pixel series is competitive for many tasks — chips are not the only road to an FM, and that matters for capstone compute budgets.

Part 4 · 10 min

Normalization statistics are model parameters

  • Per-band μ and σ — band B02 and band B08 live on different scales; SAR is already log (dB); one global μ/σ across bands destroys spectral structure.
  • Train-set-only: stats computed over test years/regions are leakage — the W2 lesson, now in the corpus itself.
  • Compute once, streaming (Welford / chunked accumulation), and version the stats file with the corpus — fine-tuning must reuse pretraining stats, not recompute.
  • Per-chip min–max normalization erases absolute brightness — a real signal (albedo, backscatter level), not noise.
Prithvi and SatMAE ship fixed per-band stats with their weights — reusing the backbone means reusing the stats
3 min. The title is the claim: μ and σ are as much a part of the model as the weights — mismatch them and a pretrained encoder sees garbage. Real-world bug to tell: fine-tuning Prithvi with your own recomputed stats silently degrades transfer; TerraTorch configs pin the original stats for exactly this reason. Lab deliverable includes per-band stats stored beside the chip store.

Part 4 · 10 min

QA masking: the curation every FM corpus lives on

  • Sentinel-2 L2A ships SCL (scene classification, 20 m): reject pixels in classes 3 cloud shadow, 8/9 cloud medium/high, 10 cirrus (+ 0/1 nodata/defective); keep 4 vegetation, 5 bare, 6 water.
  • Chip rule in today's lab: keep a chip only if its bad-SCL fraction is below a threshold (e.g. < 5–10%).
  • SMAP: use retrieval_qual_flag (bit 0 = recommended) and surface flags — frozen ground, RFI, dense vegetation, open water.
  • Skipping this step doesn't crash — it just pretrains your model on clouds. Corpus papers bury it in appendices; it is half the work.
Sen2Cor SCL classes 0–11 · SMAP L2/L3 product user guides
3 min. The last bullet is the message: curation failures are silent. Show of hands — who has trained on an image dataset without looking at 100 random samples? The chip gallery in the lab deliverable exists to force that look. Also note masks are categorical: if you ever regrid SCL, nearest-neighbor only (callback to regridding). Transition: everything today was one pipeline — one slide to close the loop.

Part 4 · 10 min

The corpus pipeline you now own

  • Choose from the zoo — knowing what's measured vs modeled, at what resolution, latency, error character.
  • Reach it cloud-natively — STAC to find, COG/Zarr to read only the bytes you need, chunking to make it fast.
  • Shape it — grid geometry (cos φ), anomalies vs climatology, the right regridding.
  • Curate it — chips with QA masking and versioned, train-only, per-band stats.
  • Every FM paper's "Data" section is these four verbs. Most of the effort — and many of the bugs — live here.

Part 5 · 15 min

The position in one slide

  • Claim: satellite data is not "images, but bigger" — it differs from natural images in ways that should change how we design, train, and evaluate models.
  • If true: EO-native architectures (SatMAE's temporal/spectral tokens, Scale-MAE's GSD encoding) are necessary, not decoration.
  • If false: general vision backbones (DINOv3) should win — and on high-res RGB benchmarks, GEO-Bench-2 says they sometimes do.
  • This tension is the course thesis; we test it empirically for the rest of the semester.
Rolf et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.01444 (ICML position paper) · GEO-Bench-2, arXiv:2511.15658
2 min framing before discussion. Be fair to both sides: the position paper is an argument, and DINOv3-beats-GFMs on RGB tasks is real counter-evidence. The honest reading: "distinct modality" is most true for multispectral, temporal, and pixel-time-series regimes — least true for high-res RGB. That nuance should come out in DQ1.

Part 5 · 15 min

Board exercise: five concrete differences

  • Collect on the board: five concrete ways satellite data differs from natural images — concrete enough to change a line of code.
  • Each must name its design consequence: tokenizer? positional encoding? masking scheme? loss? evaluation split?
  • We photograph this board and re-grade it in W7 (EO FMs) and during the async capstone sprints via GitHub.
Rule: "there's more of it" is not a difference — scale of archive vs scarcity of labels is
6–7 min. Target list to steer toward (don't reveal upfront): (1) metric scale/GSD is meaningful and varies by orders of magnitude → Scale-MAE's GSD positional encoding; (2) many physically-meaningful spectral channels and sensor types beyond RGB → SatMAE's band groups, DOFA's wavelength-conditioned embeddings; (3) revisit structure — the same place imaged repeatedly, irregularly → temporal tokens, pixel-time-series models; (4) every pixel is georeferenced → auxiliary data joins, spatial autocorrelation, non-IID splits (W2 callback); (5) planetary unlabeled archives vs scarce, geographically biased labels → SSL pretraining as the default, label-shift-aware evaluation. If students propose others (nadir viewpoint, no canonical orientation, physical units vs perceptual RGB), take them — five is a floor for discussion, not a ceiling.
