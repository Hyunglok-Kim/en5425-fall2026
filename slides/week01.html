Part 5 · 14 min

The four questions most people miss

  • C1 · backprop by hand: a=6, b=2, L=4 → ∂L/∂b = 2b = 4, so ∂L/∂z = 4, ∂L/∂x = y·4 = 12, ∂L/∂y = x·4 = 8. The chain rule is bookkeeping, done in order.
  • D2 · broadcasting: (3,1) + (1,4) → shape (3,4); C[2,1] = 2 + 10 = 12. Broadcasting stretches size-1 axes — no loops, no copies.
  • B1 · Bayes: P(drought | flag) = (0.95·0.01) / (0.95·0.01 + 0.05·0.99) ≈ 0.16. A 95%-sensitive detector on a 1% prior is wrong 5 times in 6.
  • G2 · NetCDF: (365, 721, 1440) → 0.25° global grid, one year daily; 365·721·1440 ≈ 3.8×10⁸ values (~1.5 GB at float32). You must be able to read a header and know the cost.
Full key + worked answers: course site
Two minutes each, at the board, no slides-only hand-waving for C1 — write the graph and push the 4 backwards. These four predict success better than the total: they are respectively the chain rule, array semantics, base rates, and data literacy.
